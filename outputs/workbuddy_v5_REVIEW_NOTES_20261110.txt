REVIEW NOTES - what to attack, and what is deliberately not claimed
====================================================================

Scope of the claim in this round
--------------------------------
Over the declared box (ten axes, bounds in core_v2.BOUNDS) and over the
continuous power range Q in [3000, 6000] W, the mass-flux distortion

    D_j = (m_M / A_R) / m_P - 1 ,     A_R = 0.378698225 fixed before solving

satisfies

    -18.6573 %  <=  D_j  <=  +2.6803 %        (21.3376 pp wide)

This is an OUTER BOUND (an enclosure), not an extremum.  No claim is made
that either endpoint is attained, and no claim is made that the true range is
narrower than 21.34 pp.  The earlier corner search [-10.739, -5.104] % is
strictly inside this enclosure and remains a searched value, not a bound.

Five things worth attacking, in the order that would hurt most
--------------------------------------------------------------
1. The six-slab union.
   SLABS in gbound.py are six closed 500 W intervals covering [3000, 6000].
   The union argument needs: for every Q in a slab, every accepted root of
   that slab lies inside that slab's flow bracket.  slab_window() computes
   that bracket once per slab, from corner(side,-1) and corner(side,+1) with
   the corner reduction.  Check: are the two corners really the extreme
   cases of Phi over the slab (needs dPhi/dQ > 0 and the eight signed axes),
   and does bracket() return an interval that contains ALL roots, not just
   the one its bisection converged to?  bracket() runs two independent
   monotone searches (largest m with Phi > 0, smallest m with Phi < 0) and
   returns [a, b]; if the sign pattern is not the one assumed, it should
   raise, not silently return.

2. The pairing direction inside each cell.
   cell_bound() evaluates, per cell, the low end of D_j as m_M(q_lo)/m_P(q_hi)
   and the high end as m_M(q_hi)/m_P(q_lo).  This is valid only because
   D_j increases in m_M and decreases in m_P, which is definitional.  Check
   that no cell is counted with the two roots taken at the same Q when the
   other root is allowed to move - that would be the pairing error the v7
   report itself flags.

3. Which axes are pinned and which are gridded.
   DJ_PIN = eps_M, k_aux_M, d_hole, L_HS_M are pinned to the endpoint given
   by their certified sign (they enter only the M loop, and their signs are
   certified in v4).  DJ_SPLIT = u_loss_fac, mu_fac, cp_fac, rho_fac are the
   four shared axes whose d ln r / dx straddles zero in v4, so they are NOT
   assumed monotone: they are enumerated on a k^4 tensor grid (k = 4 here).
   Attack: is any axis left free without either a sign certificate or a grid
   partition?  Count them - ten axes, four pinned, four gridded, two are
   dTin_P / dTin_M, handled separately below.  The power axis is not an axis
   here; it is the slab index.

4. The mean-value step for dTin.
   dTin is the only axis whose sign is not certified.  gbound uses
       Phi(T_in) - Phi(0) = Phi'(xi) * T_in ,  |xi| <= 2 K
   with Phi' enclosed over the same cell, so the +-2 K contributes a fixed
   additive interval instead of being evaluated as a raw interval.  Attack:
   is Phi' really evaluated over an interval that contains xi for every
   point of the cell, and is the resulting interval added with the correct
   sign?  This step is what makes the bound usable at all: a raw interval
   evaluation of the same +-2 K costs 2.5x more width on the root (and 104x
   on Phi in the v4 measurement).

5. The two silent traps.
   (a) core_v2.solve() begins with kw.pop('dtin', None).  If you pass **kw
       straight through, the two dTin axes are dropped with NO error, and
       3 kW narrows by 0.067 pp.  gbound does not hit this (it never calls
       solve), but run_v4c.py did before the fix, and a reviewer reusing
       these helpers will.
   (b) bound.bracket() returns a valid interval for any starting window,
       but its tightness depends on the window used for dPhi/dT_in inside
       the mean-value step, not on the bisection window.  Moving the
       bisection window by 0.001 kg/s changed nothing; moving the
       derivative window changed one cell from 16.0 pp to 10.2 pp.

What is NOT claimed and should not be inferred
----------------------------------------------
- No tight bound.  The k=1..4 sequence is 37.67 / 26.98 / 23.27 / 21.34 pp,
  and a W0 + C/k fit gives W0 about 4.8 pp per cell, so the enclosure can
  still be tightened by roughly 4x with more cells.  The fit is an
  extrapolation, not a proof.
- No monotonicity for the four shared axes.  That missing lemma is the only
  route from ~10 pp to ~1 pp; it is not proved here and is not assumed.
- No D_T in this round.  D_T follows from 1 + D_T = R_H / (1 + D_j); R_H was
  certified in v4 at 0.8 % width, and propagating the enclosure above gives
  a D_T interval that is dominated entirely by D_j (about 12 of its 14 pp).
  That propagation is not re-run here.
- No wall temperature, no solid conduction, no Nu correlation branch.
- No statement about physical reality.  D_j is a model-to-model distortion
  between two declared geometries; every number here is a property of the
  declared box, not a measurement.

Provenance
----------
core_v2.py sha256[:16] = f76f81934141869a, byte-identical to the copy in
v7-continuous-proof and to the frozen baseline of round 4.  No geometry,
power convention, friction model or FoM definition was changed to make the
numbers smaller.  The enclosure got tighter only by (i) replacing the raw
interval evaluation of dTin with the mean-value form, (ii) handing the
bisection a certified starting window instead of a +-12 % band, and
(iii) pinning the four M-only axes that v4 had already signed.
