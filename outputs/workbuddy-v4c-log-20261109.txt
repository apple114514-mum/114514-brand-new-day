[2] D_j: what the independent enclosure costs, and what subdivision
    recovers

  Naive: D_j in [m_M,lo/m_P,hi, m_M,hi/m_P,lo] / A_R - 1.  Valid, but it
  pairs the smallest M flow with the largest P flow, which no single
  input produces.

  Subdivided: the four M-only axes are pinned to the ends that
  extremise D_j (their signs are certified, so the pinning is exact),
  and the four shared axes are cut into k cells each.  Inside a cell
  the two flows are enclosed on the same sub-box, so the pairing is

  The width falls like width = W0 + C/k, not like a search that merely
  samples more points: every k gives a statement valid on the whole
  box, and the nested cells only sharpen it.  Fitting the four rows
  gives the cost of the next decimal place, and that number is the
  honest answer to how far this route can be pushed this round.

  power   W0 (pp)   C (pp)   k for +1 pp     cells      hours
   3000     4.783   22.194         22.2           242648          14.1
   4300     4.808   20.219         20.2           167135          10.2
   6000     4.792   19.359         19.4           140466           8.2

  W0 is the width the enclosure is converging to, i.e. the true range
  of D_j over the box; C is how much of the present width is still
  cell-size error.  C is the number that matters: to be within one
  point of W0 the grid has to reach k = C per shared axis, which is
  C^4 cells.  That is a compute task, not a method one, and it is the
  reason the certified D_j of this round is roughly five points wider
  than W0 while R_H is already tight to a fraction of a point.

  One observation worth recording: W0 is about 4.8 pp at every power,
  while the corner search of section [6] only spans 3.8 pp at 3 kW and
  3.8 pp at 6 kW.  A convergent enclosure cannot converge below the
  true range, so the corner search appears to miss roughly one point
  of it.  That is an extrapolation from four grid levels, not a proof
  of where the missing point is, but it says the searched extremes of
  earlier rounds are not the extremes.

[3] why D_j stalls: the derivative that interval arithmetic cannot sign
  For a shared axis, d ln(mM*/mP*)/daxis is the difference of two
  sensitivities that nearly cancel:
      d ln m*/daxis = -(dPhi/daxis) / (m dPhi/dm)
  dPhi/dcp_fac alone spans a factor of six across the box (it is
  -2827 to -728 Pa on the 3 kW slab for P and -4846 to -970 for M),
  so each term carries a wide interval and their difference straddles
  zero.  The table below uses the *narrowest* honest window, the
  certified bracket itself at one power, so no wider window would
  help.  The true value is far from zero, but interval arithmetic
  cannot see that without splitting.

  axis         d ln(mM/mP)/daxis, box x bracket @4.3 kW   sign

  cp_fac       [       -6.25,      +0.5559]        ZERO
  rho_fac      [     -0.1728,        +8.05]        ZERO

  The four M-only axes are certified, because for them the other
  loop's sensitivity is identically zero and the sign is just the sign
  of dPhi_M/daxis.  The four shared axes are not, and that is the
  blocking position for a corner-tight D_j bound.

[4] R_H from the loss side, and D_T through the identity
  Energy balance on the heated segment, checked independently in task 1
  to 1e-12 relative, gives Qnet = Qe - L_HS with
      L_HS = u u_fac L_HS (Tmean - T_amb).
  R_H = (Qnet_M/Qnet_P)(cp_P/cp_M) then follows from u, Tmean and the
  two inlet temperatures.  It does not use the flow solve, so
  combining it with the D_j enclosure is not circular.

   6000  [  46.4,  72.9]  [  37.0,  60.9]  [0.997559646,1.006056674]

  power  cp ratio interval            R_H interval
   3000  [0.999483903,1.000516363]      [0.996156792,1.012733719]
   4300  [0.999483903,1.000516363]      [0.996664661,1.008993057]
   6000  [0.999483903,1.000516363]      [0.997044808,1.006576165]

  The cp ratio is one to four digits.  Both loops call cp at the same
  inlet boundary and with the same multiplier, so the multiplier
  cancels exactly and the only spread is the +-2 K control error acting
  on a quantity with |dlncp/dT| about 2e-4 per K.  All of R_H therefore
  comes from the loss fraction, which is small and similar on both
  sides; that is why R_H sits within a percent of one while D_j is
  seven percent away from it.
